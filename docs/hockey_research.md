# Hockey Rink Registration: State of the Art, Data-Efficient Strategies, and a Practical Path for NHL Video with Few Labels

## TL;DR
- The single most transferable finding for your situation is **Rink-Agnostic Hockey Rink Registration (Shang et al., University of Waterloo, MMSports 2023 / arXiv:2401.01003)** — it solves exactly your problem: it trains on labelled NHL data plus synthetic rinks and unlabelled non-NHL footage, and generalizes to unseen rink types (Olympic, European) using a segmentation → homography-estimate → refinement pipeline. This is a better architectural template than the pure keypoint/pose approach of HockeyRink that is failing for you.
- Your HockeyRink replication is struggling for structural reasons, not just data volume: the public SimulaMet HockeyRink model is a **YOLOv8-Large pose model trained on only 661 SHL frames (80/10/10 split, i.e. ~529 training frames) annotated to IIHF 60×30 m dimensions** — so on NHL 61×26 m footage you face both domain shift and a template-geometry mismatch. Keypoint/pose methods are the most data-hungry and least transferable family; segmentation-of-lines-plus-geometry and synthetic-pretraining methods need far less real labelled data and generalize better.
- Recommended path: (1) build a rink **line/circle semantic-segmentation** model (robust to arena appearance) rather than sparse pose keypoints; (2) **pre-train on synthetic rink renders with domain randomization** plus transfer from a soccer/basketball calibration model; (3) use **classical CV (Canny+Hough lines, ellipse fitting for face-off circles, chamfer matching against a binary NHL template)** as a CVAT/Nuclio pre-annotator to cheaply bootstrap labels; and (4) resolve H via DLT/PnP + RANSAC with a geometric refinement step (à la PnLCalib / No-Bells-Just-Whistles).

## Key Findings

**1. The field/court registration field has largely moved away from sparse keypoint regression toward (a) dense/uniform-grid keypoints, (b) line/segment segmentation + geometric solving, and (c) synthetic-data and self-supervised training to cut labelling cost.** The dominant modern benchmark is the SoccerNet Camera Calibration Challenge; the 2023 winner (Sportlight — Nikolay Falaleev & Ruilong Chen, combined metric 0.55, ACC@5 73.22, completeness 75.59) used an HRNetV2-w48 backbone with keypoint + line detection and **57 keypoints "most of which were defined by intersecting fitting results of lines and ellipses from annotations,"** plus 8 circle tangent points and 13 points added via homography projection, then heuristic camera-parameter solving.

**2. For hockey specifically there is a small but directly relevant literature:** the classical Gupta–Little–Woodham line+ellipse method (2011), Homayounfar et al.'s segmentation+branch-and-bound (CVPR 2017, explicitly extended to hockey), the SimulaMet HockeyRink pose dataset/model (MMSys 2025), the Waterloo Rink-Agnostic pipeline (2023) and its thesis extension, and a 2025 boundary-aware segmentation method that re-annotates HockeyRink because its keypoints are "sometimes insufficient for calculating an accurate homography."

**3. Keypoint/pose methods are the most label-hungry and least arena-transferable.** They must learn to hallucinate specific named points even under occlusion, and they bake in one rink geometry. Segmentation and synthetic/self-supervised methods need less real labelled data and transfer across venues because face-off circles, blue/red lines, and goal lines exist on every rink even when their spacing differs.

**4. Classical CV is viable as a semi-automatic pre-annotator.** Canny+Hough for the straight lines (blue lines, red line, goal lines), ellipse fitting / Hough-ellipse for face-off circles, and chamfer matching of the detected edge map against a synthetic dictionary of NHL-template edge maps (the Sharma et al. 2018 approach) can produce an initial H and thus proposed keypoints for a human to correct in CVAT via a Nuclio serverless function.

## Details

### A. The SOTA landscape (2017–2026)

**Method family 1 — Keypoint / heatmap (pose-style) detection.** The workflow detects field-specific points, matches them to a 2D template, and solves H (usually DLT + RANSAC). Representative works:
- **Nie, Chen & Hamid, "A Robust and Efficient Framework for Sports-Field Registration" (WACV 2021).** Introduced the idea of a **uniform grid of keypoints across the whole field** (not just sparse corners/line intersections) plus a dense distance-map field feature, predicted jointly by a multi-task net, to handle texture-less areas. This grid idea is the backbone of most later keypoint methods.
- **KpSFR — Chu et al., "Sports Field Registration via Keypoints-aware Label Condition" (CVPRW/CVsports 2022).** ResNet-34 encoder-decoder, treats keypoint detection as instance segmentation with dynamic filter learning; uses the same uniform grid as Nie. Introduced the **TS-WorldCup** dataset — "detailed field markings on 3,812 field images from 43 videos of Soccer WorldCup 2014 and 2018 in a time-sequence fashion, which is ten times larger than the WorldCup 2014 dataset." Notable data/inference cost: ~73M parameters, ~1.5 FPS on an RTX 3090, and it needs pre-processed keypoint-identity encodings at inference; independent evaluation (Claasen & de Villiers) found it obtains the worst projection error among compared methods despite good IoU.
- **SimulaMet HockeyRink (Sarkhoosh et al., MMSys 2025)** — your reference model — is squarely in this family (see Section B).

**Method family 2 — Line/segment semantic segmentation + geometric solving (PnP/DLT/RANSAC + refinement).** This is the current best-accuracy family and the most label-efficient of the deep approaches.
- **Homayounfar, Fidler & Urtasun, "Sports Field Localization via Deep Structured Models" (CVPR 2017).** Deep semantic segmentation of grass/lines/circles, then branch-and-bound inference in a Markov Random Field; classifies lines and extracts vanishing points to cut H's DoF from 8 to 4. The authors explicitly state the method extends to **hockey and basketball**.
- **TVCalib — Theiner & Ewerth (WACV 2023, arXiv:2207.11709).** Reframes registration as **camera calibration**: a differentiable objective minimizes segment reprojection error from segmentation of known field segments, estimating actual camera pose + focal length rather than a raw H. Key advantage for you: the calibration step "does not rely on training data," only the segmentation front-end is learned.
- **No Bells, Just Whistles (NBJW) — Gutiérrez-Pérez & Agudo (CVPRW/CVsports 2024, arXiv:2404.08401)** and its journal extension **PnLCalib (CVIU 2026).** Encoder-decoder heatmaps for keypoints + line extremities; a keypoint-generation pipeline derived from SoccerNet annotations using field-line extraction and ellipse fitting; then classical DLT calibration, with PnLCalib adding a non-linear line-based refinement (points-and-lines optimization). PnLCalib reports SOTA on SoccerNet-Calibration, WorldCup 2014 and TS-WorldCup. Both have public GitHub code (mguti97/No-Bells-Just-Whistles, mguti97/PnLCalib) — the most useful modern reference implementations to adapt.
- **Sportlight, SoccerNet Camera Calibration Challenge 2023 winner** (public GitHub NikolasEnt/soccernet-calibration-sportlight; write-up at nikolasent.github.io). Two models (keypoints + lines), 57 keypoints including geometrically-derived ellipse tangent points, heuristic camera solving.

**Method family 3 — Direct homography / camera-pose regression and error-minimization.**
- **DeTone et al. deep homography** (4-corner offset regression → DLT) is the seed of this family; Zhou & Li showed direct H-parameter regression is hard due to differing parameter scales, motivating normalized parameterization (used by the Waterloo pipeline).
- **Jiang et al., "Optimizing Through Learned Errors for Accurate Sports Field Registration" (WACV 2020, Sportlogiq/UVic, arXiv:1909.08034).** Two nets: one regresses an initial H, a second regresses the registration *error* of the current warp; then optimize H by differentiating through the error net. Public inference code (vcg-uvic/sportsfield_release) — note training code is not released.
- **Shi et al., "Self-Supervised Shape Alignment for Sports Field Registration" (WACV 2022, Sportlogiq).** End-to-end self-supervised cross-modality registration trained from a natural image and its edge map with **no pairwise labelled data**, using a score-regression network for iterative refinement. Crucially it shows **pretrained weights can be fine-tuned for sports field calibration with very little data** — directly relevant to your few-shot situation.

**Method family 4 — Retrieval against a synthetic dictionary (the bridge from classical to deep).**
- **Sharma et al., "Automated Top View Registration of Broadcast Football Videos" (WACV 2018, arXiv:1703.01437).** Poses registration as nearest-neighbour search over a synthetically generated dictionary of edge-map/homography pairs, using chamfer matching (and HOG/CNN variants), with MRF + convex optimization for temporal stability. Notably data-efficient in its seeds: the dictionary was built **from only 75 manually annotated images** — "by using different permutations of pan, tilt and zoom over a set of 75 manually annotated images, we learn a large dictionary D = {I_j, H_j}."
- **Chen & Little, "Sports Camera Calibration via Synthetic Data" (CVPRW/CVsports 2019, arXiv:1810.10658).** A camera-pose engine with only three significant free parameters generates many synthetic edge images (per PnLCalib, statistics from WorldCup 2014 "were used to sample 90,000 poses which will be encoded through a Siamese Network to distinguish different edge maps"); a **two-GAN model** detects field markings in real images; initial pose is retrieved then refined with truncated distance images. This is the canonical "almost no manual annotation" method and was chosen as the base of several downstream systems because it adapts to new stadiums by changing a few hyper-parameters.

**Benchmarks & datasets:** WorldCup 2014 (Homayounfar), TS-WorldCup (Chu, 3,812 frames from 43 videos), SoccerNet-Calibration (largest, EVS-sponsored, CVPR challenges 2022–2026), CARWC (consolidated/refined WorldCup), DeepSportRadar (basketball, MMSports 2022; 480 train + 84 challenge images across 15 arenas, 3 held out for test) with the KaliCalib winner (91-keypoint heatmaps, encoder-decoder; challenge MSE 73.16 mm on the full-data model).

### B. Your reference model: SimulaMet HockeyRink — why replication is hard

Confirmed technical facts (Sarkhoosh et al., "HockeyRink," MMSys 2025, DOI 10.1145/3712676.3718338; Hugging Face SimulaMet-HOST/HockeyRink; GitHub simulamet-host/HockeyRink-MMSys2025):
- **Architecture:** YOLOv8-Large pose estimation, "adapted... to treat the rink as a single 'pose' object." Output: 56 keypoint coordinates with confidence scores. Annotation schema is YOLO pose format (one rink bbox + 56 keypoints for landmarks such as face-off dots, goalposts, and blue lines).
- **Data:** only **661 frames at 1920×1080 from SHL games**, split 80/10/10 (≈529 train / 66 val / 66 test). Number of distinct games/arenas is not stated.
- **Template geometry:** annotations use **IIHF 60 m × 30 m** dimensions (paper Figure 1 cites the IIHF rulebook), not NHL 61 m × 26 m.
- **Reported metrics:** keypoint mAP@0.5 = 97.48%, mAP@0.5:0.95 = 76.45%, precision 96.21%, recall 96.24%; 13.64 FPS on Tesla T4, 6.4 FPS on M3 MacBook Pro. No PCK, reprojection error, or homography IoU is reported — homography is shown only qualitatively.
- **Stated limitation:** the authors flag that performance "across leagues with differing rink dimensions [and] camera [setups]" is an open question. A separate 2025 paper (**Boundary-aware semantic segmentation for ice hockey rink registration**, CVIU S1077314225003509) explicitly re-annotates HockeyRink because "the provided keypoint annotations are sometimes insufficient for calculating an accurate homography matrix."

**Diagnosis of your failure mode.** You are (a) using the most data-hungry method family (pose/keypoint) with far fewer than the ~529 training frames the original had; (b) transferring across a domain gap (SHL arenas → NHL arenas differ in boards, advertising, ice logos, lighting, camera work); and (c) suffering a template mismatch (IIHF 60×30 annotations vs NHL 61×26 geometry), so even perfect keypoints would produce a biased H. Pure keypoint models must learn to place each *named* point precisely, so they need many examples of each point under many camera poses/occlusions and they are brittle when the rink layout changes.

### C. The most relevant hockey-specific solution: Rink-Agnostic (Waterloo)

**Shang, Chen, Clausi & Shafiee, "Rink-Agnostic Hockey Rink Registration" (MMSports 2023, arXiv:2401.01003)** — and the extended MASc thesis "Improving Robustness of Homography Estimation for Ice Rink Registration" (uwspace) — is the closest match to your goal. Pipeline (three separately-trained modules):
1. **Semantic segmentation** (DeepLabV3+) of rink features (face-off circles, blue lines, goal lines, etc.) — because these classes exist on every rink even when their positions differ. Trained with heavy augmentation (Gaussian noise, colour, shifts/tilts/zooms) **plus two domain-specific augmentations: copy-paste of players (to simulate occlusion) and "logo augmentation" (random text/rectangles/circle fills to teach the net to ignore arena advertising)**, and **unsupervised domain adaptation** (MIC-style EMA teacher-student with input masking) using *unlabelled* non-NHL footage.
2. **Initial homography estimator** (ResNet-18 regressing a normalized H) trained on **synthetic rinks** — they generate 200 randomly-parameterized rink templates by varying inter-feature distances (blue-line spacing, face-off-to-goal-line distance, etc.), warped by augmented NHL ground-truth homographies.
3. **Refinement module** (ResNet-18, 4-point DLT, iterable) in the Shi et al. self-supervised style.

Reported result: competitive with an NHL-only baseline on NHL rinks, and **better on non-NHL rinks — "a 1.1% IOUpart improvement on the Olympic 2014 rink and a 8.8% IOUpart improvement on the Berlin Mercedes-Benz Arena rink"** — despite having ground-truth labels for only one rink type. This is the design pattern you should emulate.

### D. Data-efficiency strategies documented in the literature
- **Synthetic rendering + domain randomization for pretraining.** Chen & Little's pose engine (90,000 sampled poses); Waterloo's 200 synthetic rinks; SoccerSynth-Field (arXiv:2503.13969) showed models *pretrained on synthetic soccer fields with randomized lighting/textures/ground patterns/camera angles* outperform real-only training for field detection, and SoccerSynth-Detection (arXiv:2501.09281) matched real data and beat it under motion blur. Directly transferable: render an NHL rink template from many camera poses with randomized boards/ads/ice/lighting to pretrain your line-segmentation net, then fine-tune on your handful of real NHL frames.
- **Self-supervised pretraining / transfer.** Shi et al. (2022) explicitly demonstrate fine-tuning from self-supervised edge-map pretraining "with few training data." Transfer from a soccer/basketball calibration backbone (NBJW/PnLCalib weights are public) is reasonable because the segmentation/keypoint front-end learns generic line/circle features.
- **Data augmentation specific to registration.** Random homography perturbations of ground-truth pairs (used by Jiang, Shi, Waterloo — and by Sharma's 75-image dictionary via pan/tilt/zoom permutations) multiply each labelled frame into many training pairs; copy-paste occlusion and logo augmentation harden against arena differences.
- **Few-shot / semi-supervised.** UDA with unlabelled target-arena footage (Waterloo MIC), and dictionary-retrieval methods (Sharma; Chen & Little) that need essentially no manual H labels.

### E. Classical (non-deep) methods as a CVAT/Nuclio pre-annotator

The classical toolbox maps almost one-to-one onto rink markings:
- **Straight lines (blue lines, centre red line, goal lines):** Canny edge detection → (probabilistic) Hough line transform, filtered by expected orientation/colour (blue vs red).
- **Face-off circles/dots:** since perspective turns circles into ellipses, use **ellipse fitting** — Hough-ellipse (skimage `hough_ellipse`) or faster modern detectors (Fornaciari-style arc grouping; least-squares conic fitting via OpenCV `fitEllipse`). Gupta, Little & Woodham (2011) is the canonical hockey precedent: they combine **point, line and ellipse features via the conic pole-polar relationship** to solve H geometrically and track sequences on the order of 1,000 frames.
- **Initial H via vanishing points:** Homayounfar's trick of classifying lines and extracting vanishing points reduces H's DoF from 8 to 4 (needs at least one pair each of vertical and horizontal lines).
- **Chamfer / distance-transform template matching:** build a binary edge template of the **NHL** rink, render a dictionary of warped edge maps over plausible broadcast camera poses (Sharma's pan/tilt/zoom permutation trick works from very few seeds), and match the detected edge map by chamfer distance to retrieve an initial H.

**How to wire this into CVAT.** CVAT supports model-assisted pre-annotation through **Nuclio serverless functions** (self-hosted, on-prem: `docker compose ... components/serverless/docker-compose.serverless.yml`, deploy with `nuctl`). Package a classical pipeline (or your best current model) as a Nuclio function that, given a frame, returns proposed rink keypoints/lines; the human then only *corrects* the proposals instead of placing 56 points from scratch. Because the classical method uses the correct NHL template, its proposals will be geometrically consistent with your target dimensions, directly attacking the template-mismatch problem. This is the standard "propose-then-correct" acceleration that makes building a few-hundred-frame labelled NHL set tractable.

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 — Fix the template first (do this immediately, near-zero cost).** Re-express all annotations and your rink template in **NHL 61 m × 26 m** coordinates. Any H solved against an IIHF 60×30 template is systematically wrong on NHL ice. This alone may explain part of your poor results.

**Stage 1 — Switch from pose-keypoints to line/circle segmentation.** Adopt the Rink-Agnostic architecture (arXiv:2401.01003) as your template: DeepLabV3+ (or a light SegFormer) segmenting face-off circles, blue lines, red line, goal lines, and the **rink boundary** (the boundary-aware CVIU 2025 paper shows adding boundary as an explicit class materially improves homography). Segmentation tolerates missing/occluded markings far better than named keypoints and transfers across arenas.

**Stage 2 — Pretrain on synthetic NHL renders with domain randomization, then fine-tune on few real frames.** Render the NHL template from hundreds of sampled broadcast camera poses; randomize board ads, ice logos, lighting, colour, and add player copy-paste occlusion and logo augmentation. Pretrain the segmentation net on this, then fine-tune on the small real NHL set you build in Stage 3. Optionally initialize from public soccer weights (NBJW/PnLCalib).

**Stage 3 — Bootstrap real labels cheaply with a classical pre-annotator in CVAT/Nuclio.** Deploy a Canny+Hough (lines) + ellipse-fit (face-off circles) + chamfer-to-NHL-template pipeline as a Nuclio function producing proposed keypoints/lines; correct them by hand. Target a few hundred NHL frames spanning multiple arenas and camera angles — comparable in scale to the ~529 frames HockeyRink used and to Sharma's 75-seed dictionary — enough to fine-tune segmentation given synthetic pretraining.

**Stage 4 — Solve and refine H geometrically.** From segmentation, sample correspondences (line intersections, ellipse tangent/pole-polar points à la Gupta/NBJW) and solve H via DLT+RANSAC, then refine by minimizing segment/line reprojection error (TVCalib / PnLCalib style). Add temporal smoothing across frames (differential H or a Kalman/Bayesian filter as in BHITK) since you have video, not single images.

**Stage 5 — Add unlabelled-arena domain adaptation only if cross-arena accuracy lags.** If Stage 4 generalizes poorly to a new arena, add MIC-style EMA teacher-student UDA using unlabelled footage from that arena (Waterloo approach).

**Benchmarks/thresholds that change the plan:**
- If, after Stages 1–2, segmentation IoU on held-out NHL arenas is high but H is still wrong → the problem is correspondence/solving, prioritize Stage 4 geometric refinement.
- If segmentation itself fails on a new arena → invest in Stage 5 UDA and more logo/appearance augmentation.
- If you cannot label even ~200 frames → lean on the fully self-supervised Shi et al. / dictionary-retrieval Chen & Little routes, which need essentially no H labels.
- If you eventually need true 3D (offside-style geometry, player height) → move from homography to full camera calibration (TVCalib/PnLCalib).

## Caveats
- Several strong methods are from industry labs (Sportlogiq) and **release inference code only, not training code** (Jiang et al. WACV 2020) or no code (BHITK) — reproducing training may require re-implementation.
- The HockeyRink homography metrics are not published quantitatively; its keypoint mAP looks excellent but a third-party paper found its annotations insufficient for accurate H, so high keypoint mAP ≠ good registration. Judge your system on reprojection/IoU metrics, not keypoint mAP.
- Synthetic-to-real transfer results (SoccerSynth) are demonstrated for soccer field *detection*, not hockey rink *registration*; the principle transfers but exact gains will differ and should be validated.
- Classical Hough/ellipse pre-annotation degrades under motion blur, heavy occlusion, and low line contrast on white ice; expect it to help most on wide, relatively static frames and to need human correction on tight/action frames.
- Rink dimensions vary even within "NHL" and "non-NHL" categories; some Finnish arenas fall between IIHF and NHL sizes, so a rink-agnostic (segmentation + adaptable template) design is safer than committing to one fixed template long-term.
- The date on one SoccerNet challenge-results reference (SoccerNet 2026) and the PnLCalib CVIU 2026 listing reflect very recent/forthcoming publications; treat their exact leaderboard numbers as provisional.
